## What we are asking you to check

This content was drafted by AI from the published pages listed below. Please check:

1. Is any red flag for this symptom **missing**?
2. Is each rule's **urgency and level of care** right?
3. Is each **question** clear, and does it mean what the source means?
4. Is the **advice** right, specific enough, and safe?
5. For each item under "AI judgement and open points": keep, change or remove?

Mark the "OK?" column, or write comments against the rule id.

## How the engine uses this

- Each rule says: when these findings are present, the outcome is **at least** this.
- The most urgent rule that fires wins. If none fires, the default outcome is used.
- Red flags are always all checked. After an emergency outcome, the engine only asks what could still raise it to the most urgent outcome (an ambulance), then stops.
- "Not sure" is never treated as "no". If a rule cannot be ruled out, the more urgent outcome is chosen.

## Advice given with each outcome

### `advice_stroke`

**What to do:** Call 999 now. You may be having a stroke, or may have had one.

**Look out for:**
- One side of the face drooping
- Not being able to lift both arms and keep them up
- Slurred or confused speech
- Signs of a stroke can stop after a short time. Get help straight away even if they have stopped.

**Get more help when:**
- Call 999 again if things get worse before help arrives

**Where to go:** Call 999. Do not drive yourself to A&E. The person you speak to at 999 will tell you what to do.

Source: [Symptoms of a stroke](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/stroke/symptoms/)

### `advice_breathing_emergency`

**What to do:** Call 999 now.

**Look out for:**
- Keep checking that the person is still responding and breathing
- If they stop responding and stop breathing, start CPR: press hard and fast in the middle of their chest. The 999 call handler will tell you how.

**Get more help when:**
- Call 999 again if the person's condition changes
- Call 999 again if you move to a different place

**Where to go:** Call 999. Do not drive yourself to A&E. Stay with the person until help arrives.

Source: [Heart attack](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heart-attack/), [When to call 999](https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/urgent-and-emergency-care-services/when-to-call-999/)

### `advice_sudden_confusion`

**What to do:** Call 999 or go to A&E now.

**Look out for:**
- Severe difficulty breathing, such as gasping, choking or not being able to get words out
- Lips or skin turning very pale, blue or grey

**Get more help when:**
- Call 999 if you get worse or cannot get to A&E safely

**Where to go:** Call 999 or go to A&E. Do not drive to A&E. Ask someone to drive you or call 999 and ask for an ambulance. Bring any medicines you take.

Source: [Shortness of breath](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/shortness-of-breath/)

### `advice_heavy_bleeding`

**What to do:** Call 999 or go to A&E now.

**Look out for:**
- Blood that comes out in spurts and is bright red and hard to control
- Blood coming through the bandage or cloth. If it does, put another one on top and keep pressing.

**Get more help when:**
- Call 999 if you cannot stop the bleeding or cannot get to A&E safely

**Where to go:** Call 999 or go to A&E. Do not drive to A&E. Ask someone to drive you or call 999 and ask for an ambulance. Bring any medicines you take.

**What you can do yourself:**
- Press firmly on the wound with a bandage or a clean, folded cloth
- If something is stuck in the wound, do not take it out. Press on either side of it.
- If the wound is on a hand or arm, raise it above your head. If it is on a leg, lie down and raise it above the level of your heart.

Source: [Cuts and grazes](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/cuts-and-grazes/)

### `advice_self_harm`

**What to do:** Call 999 for an ambulance or go straight to A&E. Or ask someone else to call 999 or take you to A&E.

**Look out for:**
- Feeling that you may be about to harm yourself

**Get more help when:**
- If your life is in danger, call 999 now
- If you need someone to talk to, call Samaritans on 116 123 at any time, or text SHOUT to 85258

**Where to go:** Call 999 or go straight to A&E. Try to be around other people and get yourself to a safe place.

Source: [Help for suicidal thoughts](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/feelings-symptoms-behaviours/behaviours/help-for-suicidal-thoughts/)

### `advice_anaphylaxis_999`

**What to do:** Call 999 now and say you think it is anaphylaxis, a severe allergic reaction. Use an adrenaline auto-injector (such as an EpiPen) straight away if you have one.

**Look out for:**
- Swelling of the lips, mouth, tongue or throat
- Struggling to breathe, or very wheezy
- Skin, tongue or lips turning blue, grey or pale
- Feeling faint, very confused or drowsy

**Get more help when:**
- If you are no better after 5 minutes, use a second auto-injector if you have one
- Call 999 again if the person gets worse or stops responding

**Where to go:** Call 999 for an ambulance. Do not drive to A&E. Stay with the person until help arrives.

**What you can do yourself:**
- Lie down. You can raise your legs. If you are struggling to breathe, sit up slowly.
- If you are pregnant, lie on your left side
- If you were stung by an insect, try to remove the sting if it is still in the skin
- Do not stand or walk at any time, even if you feel better

Source: [Anaphylaxis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/anaphylaxis/)

### `advice_rash_non_fading_emergency`

**What to do:** Call 999 or go to A&E now. A rash with these signs can mean a serious infection, such as meningitis (an infection of the layers around the brain) or sepsis (a life-threatening reaction to an infection).

**Look out for:**
- A rash that does not fade when you press a glass against it. On brown or black skin, check paler areas such as the palms, soles, inside the eyelids or the roof of the mouth.
- Being very sleepy, hard to wake, confused, or floppy
- Skin, lips or tongue that look pale, blue, grey or blotchy

**Get more help when:**
- Call 999 if the person gets worse while you wait, or you cannot get to A&E safely
- Trust your instincts. Get help straight away if you think something is seriously wrong.

**Where to go:** Call 999 or go to A&E. Do not drive to A&E. Ask someone to drive you or call 999 and ask for an ambulance. Bring any medicines the person takes.

**What you can do yourself:**
- Do not wait for a rash to appear if the person seems very unwell

Source: [Meningitis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/meningitis/), [Rashes in babies and children](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/rashes-babies-and-children/)

### `advice_sepsis_emergency`

**What to do:** Call 999 or go to A&E now. These can be signs of sepsis, a life-threatening reaction to an infection. It needs treatment in hospital straight away.

**Look out for:**
- Breathing very fast, or finding it hard to breathe
- Skin, lips or tongue that are blue, grey, pale or blotchy
- A rash that does not fade when you press a glass against it
- Being confused, very sleepy or hard to wake

**Get more help when:**
- Call 999 if the person gets worse or cannot get to A&E safely
- Trust your instincts. If you think something is seriously wrong, get help straight away.

**Where to go:** Call 999 or go to A&E. Do not drive to A&E. Ask someone to drive you or call 999 and ask for an ambulance. Bring any medicines you take.

Source: [Sepsis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sepsis/), [COVID-19 symptoms and what to do](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/covid-19/covid-19-symptoms-and-what-to-do/)

### `advice_skin_reaction_emergency`

**What to do:** Call 999 or go to A&E now. A rash with blisters, peeling skin, or sores in the mouth or eyes can be a serious reaction to a medicine or an infection. It needs treatment in hospital.

**Look out for:**
- Skin that blisters, peels or comes away easily
- Sore, swollen mouth, lips, eyes or genitals
- Wheezing, a tight chest or throat, or trouble breathing or talking

**Get more help when:**
- Call 999 if breathing gets harder or the person gets worse

**Where to go:** Call 999 or go to A&E. Do not drive to A&E. Bring any medicines you take, including any you started recently.

**What you can do yourself:**
- Do not take any more doses of a new medicine until a doctor has checked you

Source: [Stevens-Johnson syndrome](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/stevens-johnson-syndrome/)

### `advice_skin_infection_emergency`

**What to do:** Call 999 or go to A&E now. These can be signs of a serious skin infection that is spreading, which can be life-threatening.

**Look out for:**
- Pain much worse than you would expect
- Black, purple or grey patches or blisters on the skin
- Feeling dizzy or faint, confused, or a fast heartbeat

**Get more help when:**
- Call 999 if the person gets worse, becomes confused or stops responding

**Where to go:** Call 999 or go to A&E. Do not drive to A&E. Ask someone to drive you or call 999 and ask for an ambulance. Bring any medicines you take.

### `advice_rash_non_fading_urgent`

**What to do:** Ask for an urgent GP appointment or get help from NHS 111 today. A rash that does not fade when pressed, in someone who feels well, can be caused by swollen blood vessels (Henoch-Schonlein purpura), but it must be checked today.

**Look out for:**
- Starting to feel very unwell, a high temperature, a stiff neck, or bright light hurting the eyes
- The rash spreading quickly
- Tummy pain, or blood in the pee or poo

**Get more help when:**

**Where to go:** Ask your GP surgery for an urgent appointment, or call 111 or use 111 online. In an emergency call 999.

### `advice_rash_urgent`

**What to do:** Ask for an urgent GP appointment or get help from NHS 111 today. The rash needs to be checked today.

**Look out for:**
- A rash that does not fade when you press a glass against it
- A stiff neck, bright light hurting the eyes, or being very sleepy or confused
- Difficulty breathing, or skin, lips or tongue that look pale, blue, grey or blotchy
- Peeing less than usual, or a baby with fewer wet nappies

**Get more help when:**
- Call 999 or go to A&E if you notice any of these signs

**What you can do yourself:**
- Drink plenty of fluids. Babies should keep having their usual feeds.

### `advice_skin_infection_urgent`

**What to do:** Ask for an urgent GP appointment or get help from NHS 111 today. Painful, hot, swollen skin can be a skin infection called cellulitis. Early treatment with antibiotics can stop it becoming more serious.

**Look out for:**
- The redness or swelling spreading
- A very high temperature, or feeling hot, cold or shivery
- A fast heartbeat, fast breathing, or feeling dizzy or faint
- Purple patches on the skin, or cold, clammy, pale skin

**Get more help when:**
- Call 999 or go to A&E if you get any of these signs, or become confused
- Contact your GP if you do not start to feel better 2 to 3 days after starting antibiotics

**Where to go:** Ask your GP surgery for an urgent appointment, or call 111 or use 111 online. In an emergency call 999.

**What you can do yourself:**
- Raise the affected area on a pillow or chair to reduce swelling
- Take paracetamol or ibuprofen for the pain
- Drink plenty of fluids

Source: [Cellulitis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/cellulitis/)

### `advice_eczema_infected_urgent`

**What to do:** Ask for an urgent GP appointment or get help from NHS 111 today. Your eczema may be infected. Or it may be a more serious problem called eczema herpeticum. You may need medicine to treat it.

**Look out for:**
- Clusters of small blisters or open sores that spread quickly
- A high temperature, or feeling generally unwell
- Sores near the eyes

**Get more help when:**
- Call 999 or go to A&E if they get very unwell, very sleepy or confused

**Where to go:** Ask your GP surgery for an urgent appointment, or call 111 or use 111 online. Call 111 for a child under 5.

**What you can do yourself:**
- Do not scratch, and keep nails short

### `advice_rash_pregnancy_urgent`

**What to do:** Call your midwife, maternity unit, GP or NHS 111 now. A new rash in pregnancy needs checking, because some infections that cause a rash, such as chickenpox or measles, can affect your baby.

**Look out for:**
- A high temperature, or feeling very unwell
- Being near someone with chickenpox, measles or slapped cheek

**Get more help when:**
- Call 999 or go to A&E if the rash does not fade when you press a glass against it, or you feel very unwell

**Where to go:** Call your midwife or maternity unit if you have the number. If not, call your GP surgery or 111.

Source: [Pityriasis rosea](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/pityriasis-rosea/), [Chickenpox](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/chickenpox/), [Measles](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/measles/), [Slapped cheek syndrome](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/slapped-cheek-syndrome/)

### `advice_measles_urgent`

**What to do:** Ask for an urgent GP appointment or get help from NHS 111 today. Measles spreads very easily, so call the surgery before you go in.

**Look out for:**
- Difficulty breathing, or feeling more short of breath than usual
- A baby or child not feeding well, or peeing less than usual
- A high temperature that stays high after paracetamol

**Get more help when:**
- Call 999 or go to A&E for a fit, or bad trouble breathing. Also call if they cannot stay awake, get confused, go limp or floppy, or have a stiff neck. And for a rash that does not fade when pressed.

**Where to go:** Call your GP surgery before you go in, or call 111 or use 111 online.

**What you can do yourself:**
- Rest, and drink plenty of fluids
- Stay away from school, work and other people for 4 days after the rash appears
- Keep away from babies, and anyone who is pregnant or less able to fight infection

Source: [Measles](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/measles/)

### `advice_kawasaki_urgent`

**What to do:** Ask for an urgent GP appointment or get help from NHS 111 today. A high temperature for 5 days or more, with a rash, red eyes, red lips or red hands and feet, can be Kawasaki. This is rare. It needs treatment in hospital to protect the heart.

**Look out for:**
- Red, bloodshot eyes
- Dry, cracked lips, or a red, swollen tongue
- Red or swollen hands and feet, or peeling fingers and toes
- A child who is very irritable

**Get more help when:**
- Call 999 or go to A&E if the child becomes floppy, very sleepy, or finds it hard to breathe

**Where to go:** Ask your GP surgery for an urgent appointment, or call 111. Call 111 for a child under 5.

**What you can do yourself:**
- Do not give aspirin to children under 16 unless a doctor prescribes it

Source: [Kawasaki disease](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/kawasaki-disease/)

### `advice_tick_bite_urgent`

**What to do:** Ask for an urgent GP appointment or get help from NHS 111 today. A round or oval rash or flu-like symptoms after a tick bite can be an infection spread by ticks, which is easier to treat early with antibiotics.

**Look out for:**
- A rash that spreads, or looks like a bullseye
- A high temperature, a headache, aches and pains, or feeling tired

**Get more help when:**
- Ask for help again if you get pain and swelling in your joints, numbness, or heart problems in the weeks after

**Where to go:** Ask your GP surgery for an urgent appointment, or call 111 or use 111 online. Tell them if you have been in forests or grassy areas.

**What you can do yourself:**
- If the tick is still there, remove it with fine-tipped tweezers or a tick tool, close to the skin. Pull slowly upwards.
- Clean the bite with antiseptic or soap and water

Source: [Lyme disease](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/lyme-disease/)

### `advice_shingles_urgent`

**What to do:** Ask for an urgent GP appointment or get help from NHS 111 today. Shingles near the eye or nose, or with changes to your vision, needs checking quickly to protect your sight.

**Look out for:**
- Your eye becoming more red or painful
- Your vision getting worse
- The rash spreading, or you feeling very unwell

**Get more help when:**
- Medicine for shingles works best if you start it within 3 days of the rash appearing
- Call 999 if you get sudden chest pain. Call 999 if pain spreads to your arm, neck, jaw or back.

**Where to go:** Ask your GP surgery for an urgent appointment, or call 111 or use 111 online.

**What you can do yourself:**
- Take paracetamol to help with the pain
- Keep the rash clean and dry, and wear loose clothing
- Do not share towels. Keep away from babies and people who are pregnant until the blisters dry.

Source: [Shingles](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/shingles/)

### `advice_rosacea_eye_urgent`

**What to do:** Ask for an urgent GP appointment or call NHS 111 today. A painful, red or gritty eye with a red face can mean the eye is affected, which can be serious if not treated quickly.

**Look out for:**
- Your vision getting worse
- The eye becoming more painful, or bright light hurting it

**Get more help when:**
- Go to A&E if you suddenly lose sight, or the eye pain is severe

**Where to go:** Ask your GP surgery for an urgent appointment, or call 111 or use 111 online.

Source: [Rosacea](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/rosacea/)

### `advice_shingles_pharmacy`

**What to do:** See a pharmacist as soon as possible, and within 3 days of the rash appearing. They can give the same medicine for shingles as a GP. It works best when started early.

**Look out for:**
- The rash near your eye, or changes to your vision
- The rash spreading over a large area, or you feeling very unwell

**Get more help when:**
- Get an urgent GP appointment or call 111 if the rash is near your eye. Do the same if you are pregnant, or your body finds it hard to fight germs (for example during cancer care).
- Call 999 if you get sudden chest pain, or pain that spreads to your arm, neck, jaw or back

**Where to go:** A pharmacy. You can also call 111 or use 111 online.

**What you can do yourself:**
- Take paracetamol to help with the pain
- Keep the rash clean and dry, and wear loose clothing
- Do not share towels. Keep away from babies and people who are pregnant until the blisters dry.

Source: [Shingles](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/shingles/)

### `advice_rash_gp`

**What to do:** See a GP about the rash. The source gives no fixed time. If it could be scarlet fever, impetigo or another infection that spreads, call the surgery before you go in.

**Look out for:**
- A high temperature, or feeling very unwell
- The rash spreading quickly, or becoming painful, hot and swollen
- A rash that does not fade when you press a glass against it

**Get more help when:**
- Ask for an urgent GP appointment or call 111 if you get a high temperature. Do the same if the skin gets painful, hot and swollen.
- Call 999 or go to A&E for a rash that does not fade, with feeling very unwell

**Where to go:** Book an appointment with your GP surgery. A pharmacist can advise on creams and treatments in the meantime.

**What you can do yourself:**
- Put a moisturiser with no perfume on dry or itchy skin, many times a day
- Do not scratch. Pat or tap the skin instead.
- Do not share towels or flannels

Source: [Atopic eczema](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/atopic-eczema/), [Scarlet fever](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/scarlet-fever/), [Impetigo](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/impetigo/), [Psoriasis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/psoriasis/)

### `advice_rash_pharmacy`

**What to do:** Speak to a pharmacist. Many rashes are not serious, and a pharmacist can help with heat rash, ringworm, impetigo, scabies, hives and nappy rash.

**Look out for:**
- A rash that does not fade when you press a glass against it
- A high temperature, or feeling very unwell
- Skin that becomes painful, hot and swollen
- The rash not getting better, or spreading

**Get more help when:**
- See a GP if the rash does not get better after treatment, or keeps coming back
- Ask for an urgent GP appointment or call 111 if you get a high temperature or feel unwell
- Call 999 or go to A&E for a rash that does not fade when pressed, or swelling of the lips, mouth or throat

**Where to go:** Go to a pharmacy. You do not need an appointment.

**What you can do yourself:**
- Keep the skin cool, and wear loose cotton clothing
- Use an unperfumed moisturiser, and avoid perfumed soaps
- Do not scratch. Pat or tap the skin instead.

Source: [Rashes in babies and children](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/rashes-babies-and-children/)
