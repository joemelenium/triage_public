## Advice given with each outcome

### `advice_stroke`

**What to do:** Call 999 now. You may be having a stroke, or may have had one.

**Look out for:**
- One side of the face drooping
- Not being able to lift both arms and keep them up
- Slurred or confused speech
- Signs of a stroke can stop after a short time. Get help straight away even if they have stopped.

**Get more help when:**
- Call 999 again if things get worse before help arrives

**Where to go:** Call 999. Do not drive yourself to A&E. The person you speak to at 999 will tell you what to do.

Source: [Symptoms of a stroke](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/stroke/symptoms/)

### `advice_breathing_emergency`

**What to do:** Call 999 now.

**Look out for:**
- Keep checking that the person is still responding and breathing
- If they stop responding and stop breathing, start CPR: press hard and fast in the middle of their chest. The 999 call handler will tell you how.

**Get more help when:**
- Call 999 again if the person's condition changes
- Call 999 again if you move to a different place

**Where to go:** Call 999. Do not drive yourself to A&E. Stay with the person until help arrives.

Source: [Heart attack](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heart-attack/), [When to call 999](https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/urgent-and-emergency-care-services/when-to-call-999/)

### `advice_sudden_confusion`

**What to do:** Call 999 or go to A&E now.

**Look out for:**
- Severe difficulty breathing, such as gasping, choking or not being able to get words out
- Lips or skin turning very pale, blue or grey

**Get more help when:**
- Call 999 if you get worse or cannot get to A&E safely

**Where to go:** Call 999 or go to A&E. Do not drive to A&E. Ask someone to drive you or call 999 and ask for an ambulance. Bring any medicines you take.

Source: [Shortness of breath](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/shortness-of-breath/)

### `advice_heavy_bleeding`

**What to do:** Call 999 or go to A&E now.

**Look out for:**
- Blood that comes out in spurts and is bright red and hard to control
- Blood coming through the bandage or cloth. If it does, put another one on top and keep pressing.

**Get more help when:**
- Call 999 if you cannot stop the bleeding or cannot get to A&E safely

**Where to go:** Call 999 or go to A&E. Do not drive to A&E. Ask someone to drive you or call 999 and ask for an ambulance. Bring any medicines you take.

**What you can do yourself:**
- Press firmly on the wound with a bandage or a clean, folded cloth
- If something is stuck in the wound, do not take it out. Press on either side of it.
- If the wound is on a hand or arm, raise it above your head. If it is on a leg, lie down and raise it above the level of your heart.

Source: [Cuts and grazes](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/cuts-and-grazes/)

### `advice_self_harm`

**What to do:** Call 999 for an ambulance or go straight to A&E. Or ask someone else to call 999 or take you to A&E.

**Look out for:**
- Feeling that you may be about to harm yourself

**Get more help when:**
- If your life is in danger, call 999 now
- If you need someone to talk to, call Samaritans on 116 123 at any time, or text SHOUT to 85258

**Where to go:** Call 999 or go straight to A&E. Try to be around other people and get yourself to a safe place.

Source: [Help for suicidal thoughts](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/feelings-symptoms-behaviours/behaviours/help-for-suicidal-thoughts/)

### `advice_body_odour_gp`

**What to do:** See a GP about your body odour. The source gives no fixed time. A change in how you smell can sometimes be a sign of another condition, such as diabetes.

**Look out for:**
- Feeling very thirsty, or peeing more than usual
- Your skin or eyes turning yellow

**Get more help when:**
- Ask for an urgent GP appointment or call 111 if your skin or eyes turn yellow

**Where to go:** Book an appointment with your GP surgery.

**What you can do yourself:**
- Wash your armpits, groin and feet at least twice a day, and dry them well
- Use antiperspirants, and change your clothes regularly

Source: [Body odour (BO)](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/body-odour-bo/)

### `advice_body_odour_self_care`

**What to do:** You can usually treat body odour yourself. A pharmacist can suggest products that help.

**Look out for:**
- A change in how you usually smell
- Suddenly sweating much more than usual
- Body odour that affects your self-esteem and does not get better

**Get more help when:**
- See a GP if any of these happen

**Where to go:** Treat it yourself at home. A pharmacy can help.

**What you can do yourself:**
- Wash your armpits, groin and feet at least twice a day, and dry them well
- Wear clothes made of cotton, and change them often
- Do not eat too much strong-smelling or spicy food, or drink too much alcohol

Source: [Body odour (BO)](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/body-odour-bo/)

## AI judgement and open points

Everything below rests on AI judgement, not on a direct statement in a source.
