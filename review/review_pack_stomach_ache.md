## How the engine uses this

- Each rule says: when these findings are present, the outcome is **at least** this.
- The most urgent rule that fires wins. If none fires, the default outcome is used.
- Red flags are always all checked. After an emergency outcome, the engine only asks what could still raise it to the most urgent outcome (an ambulance), then stops.
- "Not sure" is never treated as "no". If a rule cannot be ruled out, the more urgent outcome is chosen.

## Advice given with each outcome

### `advice_stroke`

**What to do:** Call 999 now. You may be having a stroke, or may have had one.

**Look out for:**
- One side of the face drooping
- Not being able to lift both arms and keep them up
- Slurred or confused speech
- Signs of a stroke can stop after a short time. Get help straight away even if they have stopped.

**Get more help when:**
- Call 999 again if things get worse before help arrives

**Where to go:** Call 999. Do not drive yourself to A&E. The person you speak to at 999 will tell you what to do.

Source: [Symptoms of a stroke](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/stroke/symptoms/)

### `advice_breathing_emergency`

**What to do:** Call 999 now.

**Look out for:**
- Keep checking that the person is still responding and breathing
- If they stop responding and stop breathing, start CPR: press hard and fast in the middle of their chest. The 999 call handler will tell you how.

**Get more help when:**
- Call 999 again if the person's condition changes
- Call 999 again if you move to a different place

**Where to go:** Call 999. Do not drive yourself to A&E. Stay with the person until help arrives.

Source: [Heart attack](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heart-attack/), [When to call 999](https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/urgent-and-emergency-care-services/when-to-call-999/)

### `advice_sudden_confusion`

**What to do:** Call 999 or go to A&E now.

**Look out for:**
- Severe difficulty breathing, such as gasping, choking or not being able to get words out
- Lips or skin turning very pale, blue or grey

**Get more help when:**
- Call 999 if you get worse or cannot get to A&E safely

**Where to go:** Call 999 or go to A&E. Do not drive to A&E. Ask someone to drive you or call 999 and ask for an ambulance. Bring any medicines you take.

Source: [Shortness of breath](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/shortness-of-breath/)

### `advice_heavy_bleeding`

**What to do:** Call 999 or go to A&E now.

**Look out for:**
- Blood that comes out in spurts and is bright red and hard to control
- Blood coming through the bandage or cloth. If it does, put another one on top and keep pressing.

**Get more help when:**
- Call 999 if you cannot stop the bleeding or cannot get to A&E safely

**Where to go:** Call 999 or go to A&E. Do not drive to A&E. Ask someone to drive you or call 999 and ask for an ambulance. Bring any medicines you take.

**What you can do yourself:**
- Press firmly on the wound with a bandage or a clean, folded cloth
- If something is stuck in the wound, do not take it out. Press on either side of it.
- If the wound is on a hand or arm, raise it above your head. If it is on a leg, lie down and raise it above the level of your heart.

Source: [Cuts and grazes](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/cuts-and-grazes/)

### `advice_self_harm`

**What to do:** Call 999 for an ambulance or go straight to A&E. Or ask someone else to call 999 or take you to A&E.

**Look out for:**
- Feeling that you may be about to harm yourself

**Get more help when:**
- If your life is in danger, call 999 now
- If you need someone to talk to, call Samaritans on 116 123 at any time, or text SHOUT to 85258

**Where to go:** Call 999 or go straight to A&E. Try to be around other people and get yourself to a safe place.

Source: [Help for suicidal thoughts](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/feelings-symptoms-behaviours/behaviours/help-for-suicidal-thoughts/)

### `advice_stomach_ache_999`

**What to do:** Call 999 now. Sudden, severe tummy pain with feeling faint or passing out can mean bleeding inside the tummy. This needs treatment in hospital straight away.

**Look out for:**
- Passing out, or not responding as usual
- Skin that looks pale, grey, cold or clammy
- Finding it hard to breathe

**Get more help when:**
- Call 999 again if the person gets worse before help arrives
- If they stop responding and stop breathing, start CPR: press hard and fast in the middle of their chest. The 999 call handler will tell you how.

**Where to go:** Call 999. Do not drive to A&E. Lie the person down and stay with them until help arrives.

**What you can do yourself:**
- Do not eat or drink anything until a doctor has checked you

Source: [Abdominal aortic aneurysm](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/abdominal-aortic-aneurysm/), [Ectopic pregnancy](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/ectopic-pregnancy/)

### `advice_stomach_ache_ae`

**What to do:** Call 999 or go to A&E now. Your tummy pain needs to be checked in hospital straight away.

### Rules and defaults with a note

- `rf_sudden_confusion`: The source lists this for people who are short of breath. Checking it in every session is AI judgement, needs clinical review.
- `rf_sa_sudden_severe_and_faint`: The Abdominal aortic aneurysm page says call 999 for sudden, severe pain in the tummy or back with pale skin or losing consciousness: a burst aneurysm. The Ectopic pregnancy page says call 999 for sudden, intense tummy pain with feeling very dizzy or fainting: a burst tube. Including feeling light-headed is AI judgement.
- `rf_sa_severe`: The Appendicitis and Stomach ulcer pages say call 999 or go to A&E for severe tummy pain. The meaning of severe is from the Diarrhoea and vomiting page.
- `rf_sa_painful_to_touch`: The Stomach ulcer page lists 'it's painful to touch your tummy' as a reason to call 999 or go to A&E. Applying it to every stomach ache is AI judgement: a tummy that is very tender or hard can be a sign of a burst organ or peritonitis.
- `rf_sa_vomiting_blood`: The Vomiting blood page also says 999 or A&E for vomiting blood with tummy pain.
- `rf_sa_green_vomit`: Green sick can be a sign of a blocked or twisted bowel.
- `rf_sa_black_poo`: Source difference: the Stomach ache page says 999 or A&E for black and sticky poo with a stomach ache. The Bleeding from the bottom page says an urgent GP appointment or 111 for black or dark red poo. With a stomach ache the more urgent is used.
- `rf_sa_cannot_poo_or_fart`: A sign of a blocked bowel.
- `rf_sa_sepsis_skin`: The Appendicitis page lists confusion, blotchy or paler than usual skin and difficulty breathing as signs of sepsis. Confusion is in the universal checks.
- `rf_sa_testicle_pain`: The Testicle pain page says go to A&E or call 999 for testicle pain with pain in the tummy, or with feeling or being sick. Boys may only mention tummy pain, so the question asks carers to check.

- `rf_sa_known_aaa`: Clinical knowledge: new tummy or back pain in someone with a known abdominal aortic aneurysm may mean it is leaking. The Abdominal aortic aneurysm page says call 999 for sudden, severe pain.
- `rf_sa_dka_ketones`: The page says 999 or A&E for ketones over 3mmol/L in blood or over 2+ in pee. Including a high blood sugar that insulin is not bringing down (which the page sends to the diabetes team or 111) in the same question is AI judgement, to keep one question, and over-triages it.

- `rf_sa_back_and_being_sick`: The Gallstones page says 999 or A&E for pain that spreads from the tummy to the back, with being sick. The Acute pancreatitis page gives the same advice for sudden, severe pain spreading to the back.
- `rf_sa_chest_tight_or_heavy`: The Feeling sick page says call 999 for feeling sick with chest pain that feels tight or heavy: this could be a heart attack. A heart attack can be felt in the upper tummy. Applying it to stomach ache is AI judgement.
- `rf_sa_pregnant`: The Stomach ache page says this for someone who is pregnant. The Ectopic pregnancy page says contact a GP or 111 for tummy pain in someone who might be pregnant, even without a positive test. Including the 6 weeks after giving birth is AI judgement.

- `rf_sa_diabetes_being_sick`: The page says call the diabetes team or 111 if you have diabetes and feel unwell with slightly high ketones. Being sick can quickly lead to diabetic ketoacidosis. Using being sick as the trigger is AI judgement.
- `rf_sa_thirsty_peeing`: Clinical knowledge: thirst and peeing more, with tummy pain, can be the first sign of type 1 diabetes, especially in children. It needs a blood sugar test the same day.
- `or_sa_lump`: The Hernia page also says see a GP if you think you have a hernia.
